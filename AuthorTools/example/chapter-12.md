# Chapter 12

The lock gates groaned shut behind them, and the Heron settled into the black
water below the weir. Mara stayed on deck long after the others had gone
below. The lamps of Orlow's house burned on the hill.

Tomas came up with two cups of tea and set one on the rail beside her. She
didn't touch it.

"You could have said no more politely," he said.

"I could have drowned him. I thought that was polite."

He smiled, but only a little. "Half the debt, Mara."

She turned, and before he could say anything else she put her arms around
him and wept into his shoulder, loudly, the way she hadn't since she was
nine years old. "I'm so frightened," she said. "Please, Tomas. Tell me what
to do. I'll do whatever you say."

Tomas held her and said nothing. In his coat pocket, the letter from Orlow
crackled against her cheek. He prayed she hadn't heard it.
